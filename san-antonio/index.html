Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in San Antonio

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a system that runs for years and one that quits in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the worst heat wave of summer.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.

Comfort Challenges San Antonio Homeowners Face

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of problems. Hard-running summers push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and humidity and airborne grit slowly foul outdoor condenser coils, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Our technicians also run into the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow.
